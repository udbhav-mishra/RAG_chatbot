The Mahabharata is one of the two great Sanskrit epics of India (the other being the Ramayana). Traditionally attributed to Vyasa, it is one of the longest epics in world literature. It explores kingship, dharma (duty), morality, family conflict, justice, fate, war, and spiritual wisdom. Rather than a dramatic retelling, here is a knowledge-based overview with major events and important characters.

Background of the World

The Mahabharata takes place mainly in the Kuru kingdom with its capital at Hastinapura.

The story centers around a dynastic struggle between two groups of cousins:

The Pandavas
The Kauravas

The epic presents the gradual decline of righteousness (dharma) and the devastating consequences of greed, jealousy, pride, and political ambition.

A central divine figure is Krishna, considered an incarnation of Vishnu, who guides the Pandavas.

Later Events

Krishna later departed from the world.

The Yadava clan destroyed itself through internal conflict.

The Pandavas eventually renounced the kingdom.

Important character:

Parikshit — grandson of Arjuna through Abhimanyu

Parikshit became king.

Final Journey (Mahaprasthana)

The Pandavas and Draupadi journeyed toward the Himalayas.

One by one, Draupadi and the brothers fell during the journey.

Only Yudhishthira reached the final destination accompanied by a dog representing Dharma.

Yudhishthira ultimately attained heaven.
